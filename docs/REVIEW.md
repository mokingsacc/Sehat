# Items for Mo (doctor) to review

## Women's health and red flags (content writer notes)
- pregnancy-danger: reduced fetal movements was amber (clinic today); UK practice treats as same-day urgent, consider red. Waters breaking without labour: WHO PCPNC says go if labour not started after 6 hours. Burning urine added (not on WHO list). Check the Dari/Pashto polite term for vagina. Tip mentions husband and mother-in-law: check tone.
- pregnancy-care: "8 visits are best" (WHO 8 contacts); Afghanistan may still teach 4. Tetanus: TT 5-dose series vs Td unconfirmed; text says "ask the clinic how many doses". Iron with water not tea. Labour signs are a step, not red. Naswar uses the "no" icon.
- after-birth: BIRTH-SPACING TIP (keep or remove?): "Waiting at least 2 years before the next pregnancy is healthier... The clinic can advise on spacing births." Fever + bad-smelling discharge is urgent. Iron 3 months (PCPNC) vs 6-12 weeks (WHO 2016). Check tone of self-harm and low-mood items.
- anaemia: tea 1 hour before/after meals (no exact WHO gap found). Worm medicine "ask the clinic". Iron tablets turn stool black vs red-flags "black stool" urgent: needs reconciling. "Pale" may be said "yellow" locally.
- red-flags: suicide is a step ("stay with them, remove poisons and weapons, take them to hospital now"). Amber says "this week". Coughing blood is "this week" (TB) — decide if heavy haemoptysis should be red. Yellow eyes "this week". Local words الاشه, گیچی, شخی گردن, دریور to check.

## Everyday adult topics (content writer notes)
- first-aid: burns cooled 20 min (IFRC says at least 10; consider "keep a baby warm while cooling"). Choking "baby" = under 1 year, no unconscious-choking advice (alert sends to hospital). "Burn bigger than the person's hand" = lay rule. Added urgent item for fainting/confusion after stove fumes (CO). Dog bites says "clinic or hospital": confirm where rabies vaccine is given in Samangan. Head injury "watch for 24 hours" is a lay rule. Longest topic (~2.2 min).
- tb: "usually 6 months" (4-month regimens exist). Confirm testing/treatment still free in Samangan. Added urgent alert (heavy haemoptysis, severe breathlessness, too weak). No mention of preventive therapy for child contacts.
- blood-pressure: 30 min walking/day. Could add local salty foods (qurut, salted chickpeas/nuts). Check BP at every pregnancy visit.
- diabetes: low sugar: sweet tea or sugar water only if they can swallow, no amount. Added amber foot alert. DKA breathing not listed separately.
- stress: urgent title "tell someone now and go to clinic or hospital". Family tip added. "Avoid lots of tea in the evening". Prayer listed as a source of peace. "Notice and name" thoughts method: check it makes sense aloud in Dari/Pashto.
- hygiene: no latrine distance number (Sphere: at least 30 m). Chlorine "as the health worker shows you". Ash for handwashing (Facts for Life, weak evidence). تشناب for village pit latrine to check.

## Children's topics and vaccines (content writer notes)
- danger-child / cough: fast breathing alone is "hospital now" (IMCI treats it at the clinic with amoxicillin); consider "clinic today". Young-infant fever written as "feels hot or cold", no number. "Fever over 2 days" go back (IMCI return advice).
- newborn: chlorhexidine policy unconfirmed ("put nothing on the cord; only medicine the health worker gives"). Postnatal checks: day 1, 3, 7-14, 6 weeks (WHO 2013) vs Afghan BPHS. Skin-to-skin "father or grandmother can help": cultural check. Jaundice day 1 or yellow palms/soles = hospital now.
- breastfeeding: meal numbers per WHO IYCF. Added "no cow's or goat's milk before 6 months". Local words for porridge to check.
- diarrhoea: sunken eyes / slow skin pinch in "hospital now" (IMCI: some dehydration = clinic); consider moving. Blood in stool = clinic today. NO home-made sugar-salt ORS recipe: decide if wanted.
- fever: 37.5 C threshold, no paracetamol dose. Purple spots wording, no glass test. Measles tip.
- growth: milestone ages narrower than WHO windows (sitting 4-9 mo, walking 8-18 mo): check it won't alarm parents. "Very thin and weak" in hospital now (IMCI: outpatient if uncomplicated). Soon alert says "this week".
- vaccines: IPV2 placed at 9 months (unconfirmed). Measles only (no rubella) per 2024 WHO bulletin. OPV at 9 months left out (in 2017 not 2023 schedule). TT vs Td unconfirmed. "Never too late" softened. Vaccine names are sound-alike spellings: check against the Afghan vaccine card. PCV described as pneumonia only.

## Disease watch (community surveillance): for Mo and native speakers
Medical and public-health points (definitions are in content/src/syndromes.json, versioned; methods at /watch/methods):
- Syndromes asked in the app: suspected measles (fever + red rash all over), acute watery diarrhoea (3+ watery stools a day, no blood), bloody diarrhoea, ARI/pneumonia (cough with fast or difficult breathing), suspected CCHF (sudden fever + bleeding from nose, gums or under the skin), dog bite / rabies exposure, cough 2 weeks or more (presumptive TB), acute jaundice (recent yellow eyes or skin, not a newborn), suspected meningitis (fever + stiff neck), cutaneous leishmaniasis (sore not healed after 2 weeks).
- Defined but NOT asked: suspected malaria (no malaria page; "fever without other cause" cannot be judged by families) and acute flaccid paralysis (no page on sudden floppy weakness). Turn on ("active": true) only after adding content.
- Where the question shows: measles + meningitis on Fever; measles on Measles; both diarrhoeas on Diarrhoea; ARI on Cough; CCHF + dog bite on Illness from animals; dog bite on First aid; TB on TB; jaundice + meningitis on Danger signs in adults; leishmaniasis on Salak; and under symptom-finder results (rash, diarrhoea, breathing, cough, animals, bite, long cough, yellow, fever, sore). Check this is not too many questions on Fever and Danger signs in adults.
- Alert rules v2026-10-07.1: any single suspected CCHF (or AFP); measles 2+ in one district in one week; any syndrome 3+ in a district-week and at least 2x the mean of the previous 4 weeks. Check against what the provincial DEWS team uses.
- One phone counts each syndrome once per 14 days (undercounts several sick people in one home). After "No" the question returns after 3 days.
- Age groups: under 5, 5 to 14, 15 and over.
- Source links (WHO VPD surveillance standards page, WHO EWARN guidelines 2012, WHO fact sheets, WHO TB screening guideline 9789240022676, WHO EMRO Afghanistan situation report) were not opened from here: check they still resolve.
- Who sees the data: everyone with a dashboard key sees exact weekly counts by district and age on /watch; shareable exports hide counts 1 to 4. Decide whether viewers should only see the shareable view.
