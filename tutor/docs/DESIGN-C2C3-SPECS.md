## Rules every spec below assumes

- Tokens and sizes come only from DESIGN.md (C1). Layout uses start and end. Maths, codes and answers sit in `.m` LTR
  islands. Dari and Pashto use Persian digits on screen and on paper.
- There is a speaker on every worded element, buttons included, using P9's mechanism. Every screen also keeps P9's
  page speaker ("Hear this page") in its header. In lists, each row has its own speaker, as the mock-ups show.
  "Says" gives the line. Bands that read on open (ey, p1, p2 lesson steps, ad literacy) hear new screens and state
  changes; the others tap.
- Spoken numbers and fractions are built from recorded clips (0 to 20, the tens, hundred, thousand, "over", "point",
  "and"), and dynamic sentences from recorded template parts. Only AI text falls back to the phone's voice, and then
  to the "voice not ready" toast (DESIGN 9).
- Toasts last 4 seconds and have a speaker. In the ASCII mock-ups, `[spk]` is a speaker and `[>]` a play button.
- New shared parts: `app/js/keypad.js` (the lock's 64 px keys plus `/`, decimal point and space); `results()` and the
  ladder card (item 3); `netChip()` (item 5); the link card (item 6); the Now card and the bell toggle (items 4 and 10).
  New icons in `ui.js`: camera, bell, bell-off, external, clock, flash, download and key.

# C2

## 1. Sheet layout for photos (P13)

**Options**
- A. Corner squares to locate the page, a slot grid for fixed cells, and a QR code plus a printed code to name it.
  (Rec.)
- B. A QR code only, as both locator and name. Under perspective, cells far from the QR land in the wrong place, and
  it fails on bent paper.
- C. ArUco markers from a library (js-aruco2). Robust, but it adds a dependency and still needs a code for the seed.

**Rec: A.** Four big squares are the easiest thing for a cheap phone to find. The slot grid makes cell positions a
pure function of the item list, so HTML printed by any browser, the hub's PDF and the phone all agree. The QR saves
typing on phones that can read it; the printed code works on every phone.

**Spec**
- Page: `@page {size: A4; margin: 0}` with everything placed in mm. Print scaling, or a photocopier at 97%, does no
  harm, because the phone maps cells from the markers, not from the paper's edge.
- Markers: four solid black squares, 12 × 12 mm, with their outer edges 10 mm from the paper edges (centres at
  (16,16), (194,16), (16,281) and (194,281)). The top-right square, in every language, is a ring: it has a 6 × 6 mm
  white hole (a quarter of its area) and shows which way is up. Nothing else on a sheet may have a solid black area wider than 6 mm (a layout
  test checks this).
- Frame: the key gives every position in mm from the centre of the top-left marker (x to the right, y down). These are
  physical positions, already mirrored for the language. The frame is 178 × 265 mm.
- Header (y 10 to 30, x 26 to 184, between the top markers): the title (16 pt bold) and the tag "Maths · Year 7" at the
  start. At the end, two stacked boxes with their labels outside: "Name" (60 × 9 mm) and "Date" (36 × 9 mm), with a
  1 pt border. The name box is at least 15 mm from any cell (layout test).
- Body (y 34 to 264, x 16 to 194): items in reading order on a two-column grid (columns 87 mm wide, gutter 4 mm, row
  gap 4 mm). In right-to-left languages item 1 is at the top right; in left-to-right, the top left. Half-width items
  pair up; a full-width item takes a row; a half-width item followed by a full one leaves the other half empty. Item
  boxes have no border. The number circle is 8 mm (10 mm in bold) at the item's top start.

  | Kind | Width | Height clean / bold (mm) | Cell |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | sum, whole-number answer | half | 28 / 34 | int |
  | sum, fraction answer | half | 38 / 44 | frac (mixed if the item says so) |
  | count, up to 6 objects (12 mm pictures, rows of 3) | half | 40 / 44 | int |
  | count, more than 6 | full | 34 / 40 | int |
  | trace | full | 34 / 38 | none |
  | circle | full | 40 / 44 | none |
  | write | full | 12 + 10 per line | none (self-check) |
  | sort | full | 60 | none |
  | draw | full | 64 | none |

  P13 may move any height by up to 4 mm to fit Pack 1, and writes the final table into DESIGN.md. The working box
  under each item goes; one "Working" box fills whatever body height is left, if at least 20 mm, and is never
  photographed. A sheet whose rows need more than 230 mm fails the validator (M7-03 needs 200 mm; M1-01 in bold
  202 mm).
- Cells have a 1.4 pt black border, a 2 mm radius, and nothing inside: no lines, no dots. `int` is 30 × 22 mm. `frac`
  is two 24 × 13 mm boxes (26 × 14 in bold), 4 mm apart, with a 1.2 pt bar between them. `mixed` is a 14 × 13 mm box
  for the whole number, 3 mm before a `frac`. A cell sits at its item's end side, vertically centred, at least 4 mm
  from anything else. There is always one box per answer, never a row of digit boxes (that would tell her how many
  digits the answer has).
- Footer (y 268 to 288, between the bottom markers): an 18 mm QR code in the centre, with error correction level M, in
  alphanumeric mode, holding `Q1 M7-03-48217 PRS` (format version, code, language). Beside it, the code in 14 pt bold
  Latin characters. Nothing else: no app name, URL or cohort on learner sheets.
- Code: `<lesson code>-<4-digit seed><Damm check digit>`, so a mistyped digit or two swapped digits are refused rather
  than marked against another sheet. Sheets without a seed keep the bare lesson code. Typing accepts Persian or Latin
  digits and any separators, as check.js does today; the phone verifies the check digit and strips it before
  looking up the key.
- The key (`answer_key`) gains, for each item: `cell {kind, x, y, w, h}` (value items only), `step` (the lesson step
  for hint 1), `hints [r2, r3]` (item 3) and `prompt`. For the sheet it gains `lang`, `frame` and `code`.
- Names: `name` leaves `PrintIn`, `/api/print`, the sheet route (`sheet_page`, `sheet_html`) and `page()` (F3). The
  phone already stopped sending one (the Print screen's name toggle is gone from the working tree).
- Ink: always black on white; tracing letters 40% grey; cells and markers 100% black.
- On a phone screen: the sheet document keeps `@media screen` only for the preview thumbnail (today's scaled iframe).
  Without a printer, "Do it on screen" opens the typed check (item 3). It shows the sheet's intro and example, each
  question beside its answer box, and trace and draw items as "Do this one on paper". Online, "Get the sheet" offers
  "Print or save as PDF" (`window.print()`; the document title is the code alone, so a saved PDF is named
  `M7-03-48217`) and "Do it on screen".
- Look: bold sizes for years 0 to 6 (`body.small` today), clean from year 7.
- `make_sheets.py --batch N` uses one seed per batch by default, so a class can go over it together; `--different`
  gives each page its own seed.
- Tests (P13): every cell rectangle plus 2.5 mm stays clear of the name box, the QR code, other items' text and the
  page edge. The markers sit where stated. Chromium (print media) and WeasyPrint place every cell within 0.5 mm of
  the key's position for the same seed.

**Risks**
- A printer that clips more than 10 mm of margin loses a marker: the camera then says it can't see four squares.
- Children write past the box: crops take 2.5 mm outside the border, and the 4 mm clearance keeps neighbouring text
  out.
- Fixed slots constrain long word problems: they take full width, and anything bigger fails the validator and is
  split.
- Dropping the app name loses a little word of mouth; if paper is found, a bare code says less.

## 2. The camera and "what I read" (P14)

**Options**
- A. In-page camera with live marker detection that takes the photo itself when the sheet holds steady, a manual
  shutter, and the file input when the camera API is missing or refused. (Rec.)
- B. In-page camera with a manual shutter only: less code, but more blurred or cut-off photos and no coaching.
- C. File input only (the phone's camera app): it works everywhere, but gives no guidance, and some camera apps keep a
  copy in the gallery.

**Rec: A**, with B's shutter always on screen and C as the fallback. Children can't judge framing. A phone that says
"closer" and "hold still", then takes the photo itself, gets a usable strip the first time.

**Spec**
- Entry: Check my sheet (`#/check`). If the phone knows a sheet (printed here, opened or last checked), it shows a card,
  "This sheet?", with the title and code and two buttons: "Take a photo" (primary, camera icon) and "Type my
  answers". Other remembered sheets follow, then "Type the code" (today's code field: codes contain letters). "Take a photo" appears only when `photo` is
  on, the phone is online and consent is given. Offline, a muted line takes its place: "Photos need the internet".
- First use: one explainer screen, with a picture of a flat sheet in good light and all four squares showing. "Put
  your sheet flat, in good light. Show all four black squares." [Start the camera]. The APK's camera prompt (item 15)
  or Chrome's own follows.

```
+----------------------------------------+
| [x]       Adding fractions       [spk] |  top bar; close at the start
| +--                              --+   |
| |                                  |   |  live video; A4-shaped guide (1:1.414)
| |         live camera view         |   |  four corner brackets, 32 px x 4 px,
| |                                  |   |  --ink, each turning --good when its
| +--                              --+   |  marker is found
| Fit the four black squares in   [spk]  |  coaching line, --fs
| the corners.                           |
|                 ( O )                  |  shutter 72 px (88 bold), --accent
| [ Type my answers ]           [flash]  |  flash only if torch works and it is dark
+----------------------------------------+
```
- The camera screen sets `data-theme="dark"` on its own element, so tokens resolve dark whatever her theme.
- Capture: `getUserMedia({video: {facingMode: {ideal: 'environment'}, width: {ideal: 1920}, height: {ideal: 1080}}})`.
  Every 200 ms the phone draws the frame to a greyscale canvas 480 px wide and applies an adaptive threshold. It keeps
  dark connected regions that look square (area over convex-hull area above 0.85, hull aspect 0.7 to 1.4) and measure
  between 1.5% and 8% of the frame width, and that are either solid (no hole) or a ring (one hole holding 15% to 40%
  of the outer area). Both tests hold at any rotation. It takes the best candidate in each quarter of the frame; the
  ring gives the orientation. When all four are found and move less than 1.5% of
  the width over three checks, it takes the full-resolution frame, vibrates for 30 ms and plays the shutter click.
- Processing, all in memory:
  - Find the markers again at full resolution, then map the four centres to the frame's mm.
  - Read the QR area with `BarcodeDetector` where it exists. If it names a different sheet from the one chosen,
    switch silently and say "This is sheet M7-03-48217". If no sheet was chosen and no QR was read, ask for the code
    in the code field and keep the photo in memory.
  - Turn each value cell's rectangle, plus 2.5 mm, into a greyscale tile of 280 × 200 px (scaled to fit, centred,
    with a white surround).
  - Check each tile for sharpness (variance of the Laplacian, threshold set from the fixtures), glare (under 5% of
    pixels at 255) and darkness (mean above 60).
- Strip: tiles in item order, two per row. Each tile has a 24 px black band on top with the item number in white Latin
  digits. The strip is 600 px wide (two 280 px tiles, 13 px gutters), JPEG quality 0.7. It never contains the name
  box, the header or the QR code. The frame and the strip live only in variables: never `localStorage`, IndexedDB,
  the Cache API, a file, a download link or a console log. Every reference is dropped when the screen closes.
- Quality fails: an "Is this clear?" screen shows the flattened sheet (from memory) with the cells outlined in
  `--accent`, 2 px, and the bad ones in `--bad` with their numbers. It says "Some boxes are blurry", "too dark" or
  "too shiny", with "Retake" (primary) and "Use it anyway" (outline). When quality passes, this screen is skipped.
- Reading: `POST /api/m/photo/mark {code, seed, lang, strip}` returns `[{n, read}]` and no marks. The server sends
  `read_sheet` the strip and, for each tile, only its number, its cell kind (int, frac or mixed) and the expected
  digits (Persian for prs and ps, Latin for en), never the key's answers: a model that knows the answer "reads" it
  into a blurred box. The phone marks with `numbers.js` against the cached key, the same code as typed checks, so the
  server never learns right or wrong. (This changes BUILD-PLAN 3.5's description of the route.)
- While it reads: the flattened sheet stays on screen, dimmed to 40%. Over it, a 96 px ring fills over 15 seconds and
  holds at 90% until the reply (the consent ring's component; a static ring under `prefers-reduced-motion`), with the
  line "Reading your answers…".

```
+----------------------------------------+
| <  Here's what I read            [spk] |
| Is it what you wrote?            [spk] |
| 1 [tile: her writing]  3/4+2/4   5/4  Fix
| 2 [tile]               1/5+2/5   3/5  Fix
| 3 [tile]               2/6+3/6    ?   Fix   "I couldn't read it" (--warm-ink)
| 4 [tile]               1/3+1/3  Empty Fix   (--muted)
| [ Yes, mark it ]          [ Retake ]   |  bold: "Check it!"
+----------------------------------------+
```
- "What I read": each row shows the item number; her tile (120 px wide clean, 160 bold; `--r` radius, `--line`
  border); the question from the key (`--muted`, `--fs-sm`); the reading (`--fs-h1`, bold, in her digits); and a
  "Fix" link that opens the keypad. A fix replaces the reading. Fixed answers are marked on the phone like the rest.
- File-input fallback: used when `getUserMedia` is missing or refused, or when no frames arrive within 5 s of
  permission. `<input type="file" accept="image/*" capture="environment">` sits behind the same "Take a photo"
  button, after the explainer picture and the line "Take the photo from above, with all four black squares showing".
  The photo is decoded with `createImageBitmap(file, {resizeWidth: 2000})`, honouring EXIF orientation, and goes
  through the same pipeline. If the markers aren't found: "I couldn't find the four squares", with "Try again" and
  "Type my answers". After a fallback photo, one muted line: "Your phone may keep this photo in its gallery; you can
  delete it there."

| State | Shows | Says |
|---|---|---|
| open | guide and coaching | "Put your sheet flat in good light. Show all four black squares." |
| no squares for 3 s | | "Fit the four black squares in the corners." |
| 1 to 3 squares | found corners turn green | "Move back a little" (a found square touches the edge), else "Show all four squares." |
| moving | | "Hold still." |
| dark | flash button | "It's dark. Turn on the light." |
| captured | vibrate, click | "Got it!" (child tone), "Photo taken" (adult tone) |
| quality fail | Is this clear? | "Some boxes are blurry. Take it again." |
| reading | ring | "I'm reading your answers." |
| 10 s | | "Still reading…" (once) |
| 30 s | Keep waiting, Type my answers | "This is taking a long time." |
| connection lost or error | Try again, Type my answers | "The internet stopped. Your photo was not kept." ("Try again" resends the strip while the screen is open.) |
| AI budget spent | Type my answers | "Photo checking is resting today. Type your answers instead." |
| nothing readable | Retake, Type my answers | "I couldn't read this photo." |
| read | What I read | "Here's what I read. Is it what you wrote?" Each row: "Number 3: I read five over four." |

- Coaching shows one line at a time. It is spoken only when a state has lasted 1.5 s, and never more often than once
  every 6 s.
- Right to left: the top bar mirrors (close at the right in Dari and Pashto). Tiles and the strip are images and are
  not mirrored. Rows start with the number at the start side.
- Bands: p2 and ad literacy get bold sizes and spoken coaching; s1, s2 and ad readers get clean sizes, and tap to
  hear. p1 and ey have no photo (p1 types its answers with a grown-up). The hub has no photo (no internet, no vision
  model) and keeps typed checks.

**Risks**
- Handwritten Persian digits: a Latin-style "0" looks like Persian ۵ (5), and Persian ۰ is a dot that children may
  leave out. The prompt names the digit system, and "what I read" shows ۵ where she meant 0, so she can fix it. The
  blind test (P16) measures digits first.
- Slow phones: if one detection pass takes more than 150 ms, check every 500 ms instead and make the shutter the main
  way.
- `BarcodeDetector` is missing in some browsers: the remembered sheet and the printed code cover it.
- Pilot reach: if Pack 1 is p1 only, the pilot has no photo users (p1 has no photo upload).

## 3. Results and the hint ladder (P15)

**Options**
- A. One results screen for typed and photo checks; readings are confirmed first; three rungs; no answer shown for
  value items; homework goes through Ask's ladder, with "Check my answer". (Rec.)
- B. Show marks at once, with "That's not what I wrote" on each row: one screen fewer, but misreads look like her own
  mistakes.
- C. Show the answer after rung 3: simpler, but it breaks "hints, never answers" and the evidence behind it.

**Rec: A.** It feels fair because:
1. She fixes misreads before anything is judged.
2. She knows how many hints there are ("Hint 1 of 3").
3. Hints cost nothing: right after a hint is still right.
4. She can try again as often as she likes.
5. The last rung is very close to the answer, and "More like this" gives fresh items.
6. "Something looks wrong?" lets her challenge a mark.

**Spec**
- One renderer, `results()` in `screens/check.js`, serves typed checks and `photo.js`.

```
+----------------------------------------+
| <  Adding fractions              [spk] |
| [ * * -   6 right! ]             [spk] |  --good-soft / --good-ink; adult: "Correct: 6 of 8"
| 1  5/4                              v  |  right: collapsed, --good mark
| 2  4/8   Look again                 x  |  --bad
|    [ Hint ]  [ Try again ]             |  Hint: --warm-soft fill, --warm-ink, --warm border
|    +- Hint 1 of 3 ------------- [spk] -+
|    | Look at step 2 of the lesson:     |  --fs-read, reading font
|    | "Same bottom numbers"   [Open]    |
|    +-----------------------------------+
|    [ Another hint ]  [ Try again ]     |
| 5  -     Not done      [ Type it ]     |  --muted
| 7  (write) Done it on paper? [Compare] |  self-check, as today
| Added to your plan: Adding fractions [spk]
| [ More like this ]         [ Done ]    |  two actions at most
| Something looks wrong?                 |  muted link
+----------------------------------------+
```
- Score: in the child tone, three stars for 90% or more right first time, two for 60% or more, else one, with "6
  right!". In the adult tone, a check mark and "Correct: 6 of 8".
- Rows: right is a ✓ (the plain mark, whether or not hints were used). Wrong shows "Look again", "Hint" and "Try
  again" (the keypad; marked on the phone against the cached key). Unanswered shows "Not done" and "Type it".
  Self-check items (write, circle, sort) keep "Done it on paper? Compare", then "Was it right? Yes / No", because
  there is nothing to hint at in a circled picture. Trace and draw items say "For a grown-up to look at".
- Rungs for Qalam sheets are generated by code with the key, cached on the phone and checked for leaks when the key
  is made (5.3):
  1. "Look at step N of the lesson: '<step title>'", with "Open that step" (`#/lesson/<id>/<step>`; Back returns to
     the results).
  2. A worked example with other numbers, from `hints()` beside each generator. Fixed pack items carry their own
     `hints`, and the validator requires them.
  3. The first step for this item: "Both bottom numbers are 4, so the answer's bottom number is 4. Now add the
     tops."

  A rung that fails the leak check becomes "Listen to this step of the lesson again".
- After rung 3 and another wrong try, the row says "You're close. Try it again on paper." and offers "More like this"
  (a fresh sheet with a new seed). In cohorts with a teacher it also offers "Ask my teacher", which sends the sheet
  code, the item and her answer as a generated question. The first time a learner ever reaches rung 3, one line,
  spoken: in the child tone, "I won't tell you the answer — you can find it!"; in the adult tone, "Qalam gives hints,
  not answers: working it out is what makes it stick."
- No "Show answer" for value items, typed or photographed. This removes `show_answer` from check.js. The answer page
  stays with the teacher or guardian, behind the PIN.
- "Something looks wrong?" lets her pick an item, then sends `check.dispute {object: lesson:n}` (no text) and says
  "Thank you. A teacher will look at this item."
- Events: `check.done` (as today); `check.item {object: lesson:n, value: 0 wrong, 1 right first time, 2 right after
  hints}`; `hint.rung {object: lesson:n, value: 1 to 3}`; `check.dispute`.
- Homework mode: module `homework`, bands p2, s1 and s2, in cohorts with AI on for her language. It adds no new
  screen. "Homework help" on Home opens `#/ask?hw=1`: the title is "Homework help", the placeholder "Type the
  question from your book", with optional subject chips for her band.
  - In Ask, from either door, the brain sends exercise-like questions (a sum, "solve", "find", "how many", a
    textbook exercise) to the ladder. Concept questions ("what is", "why") get an explanation with its source badge,
    never the answer to an exercise. When in doubt, the ladder.
  - The model solves privately, twice. Hints 1 to 3 are as above (rung 2 uses a generator when one matches, else the
    model), each checked against the private answer in every form.
  - Under the ladder, "Check my answer": she types her answer, and the server compares it with the private answer
    when both runs agree. It replies "Yes, that's right" or "Not yet: try the next hint". Three checks per question.
    If the runs disagree: "I'm not sure about this one. Ask your teacher."
  - With hold-for-teacher on, the whole ladder waits for the teacher as one item: "Your teacher will look at this
    first", with the waiting badge.
- States: typed checks work offline (the key and hints are cached). With no cached key: "Connect once to get this
  sheet's answers". Homework help offline: "Homework help needs the internet", and Ask's offline answers work as
  today. AI budget spent: "Hints are resting for today", with a lessons search.
- Right to left: rows mirror; sums and answers sit in `.m`.
- Bands: p2 gets bold sizes and hints read out as they appear; the s-bands and ad tap. Homework help is for p2, s1
  and s2 only.

**Risks**
- Without "Show answer", self-learners with no teacher may stall. The close rung 3 and "More like this" soften it;
  watch the rung-3 and dispute rates in the pilot.
- Guessing through "Check my answer": three tries per question.
- The leak check misses spelled-out answers in Dari or Pashto ("three quarters"): word lists from the reviewers in
  January, and teachers see held ladders during the pilot.

## 4. Home for in-school and out-of-school learners

**Options**
- A. Ask once, on Home: "Qalam can be your school." (Rec.)
- B. Let the cohort decide (school day on for the pilot cohort; off for `open`, with a switch in Settings): no
  question, but out-of-school learners in `open` never get the default.
- C. Ask during sign-up: it breaks "tap a year and learn".

**Rec: A.** One tap, at the moment it matters, keeps the school day on by default for those who want it. The phone
stores a feature switch she chose, not a statement about her.

**Spec**
- The question is the timetable module's `home.action` offer: role secondary, top priority, shown when the band is p1
  to s2, a timetable is cached and `day` is unset.

```
+- Your school day -------------------- [spk] -+
```

- New cohort: a random id, bands and languages, and a neutral label. The label is refused if it contains "girl",
  "boy", "female", "women", "out of school", "banned" or their Dari and Pashto forms (F4). The join code is shown once
  and stored hashed. "Class cards" prints an A4 page of eight cards (QR and code, nothing else).
- Content:
  - Packs, each with its version, its lessons, sign-offs per role ("Dari 10/10 · Pashto 8/10 · subject 10/10") and
    its release per cohort.
  - "Publish" works only when every lesson is signed and its hash matches; "Roll back" returns to the previous
    version.
  - Review notes from P17.
  - The wishlist across cohorts (text, count, bands, first seen), with "Planned" and "Not for Qalam".
- AI spend: per cohort and day, the tokens, an estimated cost (from a price table in config), calls by purpose and
  held answers. Fourteen days of bars (`--accent`); a `--warm` tag at 80% of the cap.
- Safety: daily counts per cohort of rules hits (distress, health, politics, religious rulings, weapons); the oldest
  held item, in hours; leak-check rejections; disputes per lesson item. Counts only; the text stays with the teacher.
- Staff: emails, roles (teacher; reviewer for Dari, Pashto, subject, scholar or adult; admin) and cohorts.
- Log: every change, filtered by cohort, kept for two years.
- States: offline shows "Connect to manage Qalam". A failed save changes nothing and says so.
- Right to left: in Dari, tables and menus mirror; codes and numbers sit in `.m`.

**Risks**
- One stolen admin login means full control. Access requires a second factor; production deploys still need
  approval in GitHub.

## 13. The review screens (P17)

**Options**
- A. `/review/`: a queue per role; each lesson step by step in the learner's own look, with its recording; OK or
  Change per step, with chips; holding a button signs the content hash. (Rec.)
- B. One verdict per lesson: faster, but comments lose their step and changed steps can't be pointed out.
- C. Paper only: slow round trips. Keep paper as the backup.

**Rec: A.**

**Spec**
- `app/review.html` at `/review/`, on staging (the reviewer cohort), with Access by reviewer email and the role from
  `staff`.
- Queue: "Lessons for you", grouped by pack. Each row has the title, the code and a status tag: Not started, Step 4 of
  9, Changes asked, Signed, or Changed since you signed (`--warm-soft`, `--warm-ink`).

```
+----------------------------------------+
| <  M7-03 Adding fractions . Dari       |
| Step 3 of 9            (changed)       |
| +------------------------------------+ |
| | the step drawn by the real lesson  | |  framed 360 px box, in the lesson's
| | renderer, in the s1 look           | |  band look
| +------------------------------------+ |
| [ Play the recording ]                 |
| [ OK ]                 [ Change ]      |  OK: --good-soft, --good-ink, --good border
| (spelling) (Afghan word) (maths)       |  chips appear after Change
| (picture) (audio) (too hard) (unsafe)  |
| [ comment                          ]   |
| [ Next ]                               |
+----------------------------------------+
```
- On a step with a recording, OK unlocks only after the clip has played once. A step that needs a clip but has none
  ("No recording yet": every ey, p1 and letter step) blocks signing.
- The last steps show the worksheet (the A4 preview and its key, hints included), the link cards and the source
  note.
- Signing: when every step is OK, "Sign this lesson" shows the short hash (8 characters), the role, the initials
  (typed once per reviewer and kept in `staff`) and the date. "Hold to sign" (the consent ring, 3 s) writes the
  `signoffs` row. If any step is "Change", the button becomes "Send my changes".
- Changed lessons: steps whose hash changed carry "changed", and "Next changed step" skips the rest. Unchanged steps
  show "OK last time". The signature covers the whole new hash.
- The content hash covers the lesson's text, pictures and audio.
- Comments go to `review_notes (lesson, step, role, chips, text, status, at)` and show in the admin's Content view.
- Paper: "Print for review" lists the steps with tick boxes, a comment line and the short hash. The content lead
  enters it, marked "signed on paper".
- States: offline shows "Connect to review". Right to left: the Dari interface mirrors, and the step keeps its own
  direction.

**Risks**
- Reviewers might sign without listening: OK unlocks only after the clip has played.

## 15. The Android app: download screen and permission prompts (P23)

**Options**
- A. A `/get` page with three steps shown in pictures, and the APK served from the Worker. Inside the app, each
  prompt comes at first need, after a short explainer. Exact alarms come through `USE_EXACT_ALARM`, with no prompt.
  (Rec.)
- B. A link to the GitHub release page: in English, confusing, and a third party sees every download.
- C. Facilitators install by USB only: the pilot's practical path anyway, but not enough after it.

**Rec: A for everyone, plus C for the pilot.**

**Spec**

```
+----------------------------------------+
| Qalam for Android                [spk] |
| Works without internet. Can ring [spk] |
| your school bells.                     |
| [ (download) Download . 18 MB ]  [spk] |  primary; the size is an example
| Version 1.2                            |  plus the shell's minimum Android version
| 1 [picture] Open the file        [spk] |
| 2 [picture] Allow "Install       [spk] |
|   unknown apps" for Chrome             |
| 3 [picture] Tap Install          [spk] |
| If a warning appears, tap "More  [spk] |
| details", then "Install anyway".       |
| For teachers: check the file  >        |
+----------------------------------------+
```
- The page is plain, in the clean look and the reader's language.
- The APK is a Worker static asset (`/get/qalam-<version>.apk`, same origin, type
  `application/vnd.android.package-archive`) while it stays under the 25 MiB per-file limit; above that, the GitHub
  release asset. "For teachers" shows the SHA-256 and the signing certificate's fingerprint.
- Links to `/get`: Settings → About → "Get the Android app" (in Android browsers only), and the printed class card.
  Never on Home.
- Updates: `/api/hello` carries `apk {latest, min}`. A newer version adds a Settings row, "Update Qalam". Below
  `min`, one full-screen card, "Please update Qalam", with the Download button. The app never installs its own
  updates (no `REQUEST_INSTALL_PACKAGES`).
- Manifest permissions: `INTERNET`, `CAMERA`, `POST_NOTIFICATIONS`, `USE_EXACT_ALARM`, `SCHEDULE_EXACT_ALARM` with
  `maxSdkVersion="32"`, `RECEIVE_BOOT_COMPLETED` (to restore alarms) and `VIBRATE`. None for storage, microphone,
  contacts or location.
- Each permission is asked at first need. An explainer comes first: a picture, two lines, a speaker, "Continue" and
  "Not now".

| Permission | When | Explainer | If refused |
|---|---|---|---|
| Camera | the first "Take a photo" | "Qalam uses the camera to read your answers. The photo stays in the phone's memory and is not saved." | "Type my answers"; on the Check screen: "The camera is off. You can allow it in Settings." |
| Notifications (Android 13 and up) | after "This phone is mine" | "Qalam can ring your bells when it is closed. The notification only says 'Qalam'." | alarms off; the School day row says "Allow notifications to ring bells" |
| Exact alarms | never asked | | if `canScheduleExactAlarms()` is false, inexact alarms (`setWindow`, 5 minutes), silently |
| Battery optimisation | never asked | | the facilitators' tip sheet per brand |

- "This phone is mine" is asked in the APK only, right after "Yes, Qalam is my school": "Is this phone yours?" (with a
  speaker), "Yes, it's mine" or "No, I share it". Yes leads to the notification explainer. No says "Bells will ring
  only while Qalam is open." She can change it in Settings → School day.
- WebView: grant `RESOURCE_VIDEO_CAPTURE` only to the app's own origin. External links open the system browser.
- The launcher name is "Qalam", with today's icon. No disguised name or icon: being found with a disguised app is
  worse.

**Risks**
- Google's developer verification. It has been enforced since 30 September 2026 in Brazil, Indonesia, Singapore and
  Thailand, and goes global during 2027. An unregistered app then installs only through the "advanced flow"
  (Developer options, sign-in, restart, a 24-hour wait), which learners can't use. Limited Distribution Accounts
  cover only 20 devices. Installs over ADB are exempt, so facilitators can install from the hub laptop by USB.
  Qalam's package and signing key must be registered by a verified developer before the rollout reaches Afghanistan.
  Whose identity that uses (Mo's or the CIO's) is a new decision for Mo.
- Play Protect warnings frighten guardians: the page shows the exact taps, and facilitators install in class.
- Phone makers that stop alarms (item 10).
